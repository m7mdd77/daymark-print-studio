# When one role goes off

## A bad Sales promise

Choose **Sales makes a bad promise** before starting a week. The normal paper delay remains, and Sales still makes a genuine independent call. The failure injector then replaces its proposed actions with a $90 same-day, 120-sheet premium order. That action is clearly recorded as injected; it is not attributed to DeepSeek.

The Sales validator refuses the order because it loses money at the stated costs. No order, cash, invoice or inventory is partially changed. The rejected promise becomes part of the shared company record. Production independently reads the remaining work and can protect it with an express paper purchase and a valid press job. Finance independently reads the rejection and can escalate the unsafe enquiry to the owner. The original genuine Sales plan is retained separately for honest evidence.

The owner can decline the enquiry or approve a smaller standard-paper order at a capacity-safe date and viable price. That event updates the same ledger and is visible to all subsequent role calls. Models cannot approve their own exception.

## A role cannot return a usable plan

There is no retry or canned replacement. The role failure is committed, the owner is notified and other roles continue from the real shared records. Production cannot make an invoice appear. Finance cannot bill queued work. A later independent Finance reading can find printed-but-uninvoiced work and catch up once, without duplicating a prior payment.

The real local Tuesday run exhibited this path: Production printed the gallery order using Monday's express purchase, while Finance returned no usable plan. The order remained printed and uninvoiced; revenue stayed at $240. The UI raised a Finance owner review. This is actual provider/browser evidence in `evidence/local-tuesday.json`, distinct from injected tests. On the actual local Wednesday run, Finance independently found D102 still uninvoiced and issued its invoice together with D103 and D201. It did not collect those new invoices in that plan; those receivables remained open. Sales also proposed a duplicate escalation of a resolved enquiry, which was refused without reopening it. See `evidence/local-recovery.json`.

## Other refusal and recovery paths

Automated tests cover missing/duplicate order IDs, role scope expansion, hidden approval fields, insufficient paper, excess press capacity, cash reserve, below-floor quotes, billing unfinished work, repeat invoice/payment, stale commands, multi-record atomicity, cross-origin/CSRF requests, unauthenticated worker access, cross-company job IDs, expired/repeated completion, concurrent allowance admission, reversed polls, interrupted-worker recovery and ledger tampering.

Those tests use explicitly labeled doubles. They establish the coded invariants, not model correctness. Numeric and scope checks cannot prove that every accepted business choice is optimal. Hash chaining is not a signature or a tamperproof guarantee against an operator controlling the whole database.
