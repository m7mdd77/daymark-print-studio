# The autonomous company needs a shared reality

In two years, the useful autonomous company will look less like a meeting of simulated employees and more like a small operating system for commitments.

Sales creates a promise. Production consumes capacity to fulfil it. Finance turns completed work into a claim for payment. These are different responsibilities over the same business, not three conversations with similar prompts. Their disagreements are valuable only when they meet at a durable record that someone can inspect.

The scarce resource will not be generated plans. It will be trustworthy coordination: what changed, who had authority, which assumption failed, and which commitment now needs a person. A model should be free to notice a paper shortage and choose an affordable correction. It should not be free to declare its own purchase budget, rewrite an invoice amount or approve an impossible promise.

Human work will concentrate at the boundaries: accepting a weaker margin for a strategic customer, renegotiating a deadline, changing risk limits, and resolving evidence that does not fit the rules. An inbox should explain the concrete tradeoff rather than request a vague approval of “the AI’s plan.”

The strongest products will make business history replayable without paying models to reconstruct it. If a company cannot explain its revenue or recover when one role fails, adding more agents makes it less autonomous in any useful sense.

Autonomy becomes credible when routine work closes its own loop and exceptional work arrives with an accountable choice.
